# Ticket #008 - Detection Health Assessment

## Purpose

Assess the current authentication detection before making any tuning changes. The objective is to improve useful security signal per unit of analyst attention without creating unacceptable loss of detection coverage.

## What We Currently Know

- The detector monitors authentication activity.
- It generated more than 600 alerts during the previous week.
- SOC analysts report that most alerts appear to be routine authentication noise.
- Analysts are beginning to skim or ignore alerts from this detector.
- The detector cannot simply be disabled.
- Reducing alert count alone is not considered success.
- Any tuning must preserve meaningful attack visibility.

## What We Do Not Know

We do not yet know:

- the total number of authentication events processed;
- the exact existing detection logic;
- the threshold or aggregation window;
- which users generate the most alerts;
- which source systems generate the most alerts;
- how many alerts are duplicates;
- how many alerts represent legitimate user mistakes;
- how many alerts correspond to suspicious activity;
- whether privileged accounts are treated differently;
- whether source familiarity is considered;
- whether successful authentication after failures is considered;
- whether distributed authentication attempts are detected;
- how much analyst time is consumed;
- the existing false-positive rate;
- the existing false-negative rate;
- which attack patterns the detector may currently miss.

## What 600+ Alerts Tells Us

The alert count demonstrates that the detector is producing a high operational workload.

It also suggests that analysts may be receiving substantially more authentication alerts than they can meaningfully investigate.

## What 600+ Alerts Does Not Tell Us

Alert volume alone does not establish that the detector is poorly designed.

It does not tell us:

- whether the underlying authentication volume is unusually high;
- whether the alerts are mostly true positives or false positives;
- whether alerts are duplicated;
- whether several alerts represent the same security incident;
- whether a small number of entities create most of the volume;
- whether reducing the volume would remove important attack visibility;
- whether suspicious activity is currently being missed.

Therefore, alert count by itself is not an adequate tuning metric.

## Information Needed to Determine Detection Health

The assessment will measure:

1. Total authentication events.
2. Total alerts generated.
3. Unique users associated with alerts.
4. Unique source systems associated with alerts.
5. Alerts per user.
6. Alerts per source.
7. Duplicate or redundant alerts.
8. Alerts associated with known benign scenarios.
9. Alerts associated with known suspicious scenarios.
10. Suspicious scenarios missed by the detector.
11. Privileged-account alert coverage.
12. Distributed-source attack coverage.
13. Estimated analyst investigation workload.
14. Detection latency where measurable.

## Success Criteria

The revised detector must demonstrate improvement against the same test dataset used for the baseline.

### Security Guardrail

All intentionally suspicious scenarios detected by the original detector should remain detectable unless a documented compensating detection provides equivalent or better visibility.

Any lost detection coverage must be explicitly identified and evaluated.

### Alert Quality

The revised detector should reduce alerts caused by known benign or redundant activity while retaining alerts associated with meaningful suspicious behavior.

### Duplicate Reduction

Repeated alerts representing the same underlying authentication behavior should be substantially reduced without hiding escalation or material changes in activity.

### Analyst Actionability

A larger proportion of alerts should contain enough context to support an analyst decision or clearly justify additional investigation.

### False-Positive Reduction

The percentage of alerts generated by known benign scenarios should decrease relative to the baseline.

### True-Positive Retention

Suspicious scenarios detected by the baseline detector should remain visible after tuning unless the loss is documented and compensated for by another signal.

### Workload Improvement

Estimated analyst review effort should decrease because fewer redundant or low-value alerts require individual investigation.

### Attack Coverage

The revised detector must still provide visibility into multiple authentication attack patterns, including:

- repeated failures;
- privileged-account activity;
- unusual-source activity;
- successful authentication following suspicious failures;
- repeated activity from one source;
- distributed activity from multiple sources.

## Primary Evaluation Question

The final comparison will answer:

"Did the revised detector improve security signal and analyst usefulness, or did it merely become quieter?"

## Current Status

Phase 1 assessment established.

No detector tuning has been performed.
No thresholds have been changed.
No users or networks have been excluded.
No suppression rules have been implemented.
